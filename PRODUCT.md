# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: Portland locals deciding whether to visit Ember & Bean. They arrive (often on a phone, often close to the moment of going) to check the menu, prices, hours and address, and to decide if this is their kind of place.

## Product Purpose

Marketing and menu site for Ember & Bean, a neighborhood coffee shop serving small-batch roasted coffee, house-baked pastries and a place to sit. Success is a local choosing to walk in, and over time ordering ahead or buying beans online.

## Positioning

A neighborhood roaster with a known address: small-batch roasting, beans bought directly from named partner farms, everything baked in-house, and a shop with a corner table to settle into.

## Operating Context

Single physical location (128 Maple Street, Portland, OR). Weekly opening hours, a short menu in four categories (coffee, tea, pastries, bites) with prices, and contact via phone, email and Instagram (@emberandbean).

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Stack: Astro 7 with Tailwind CSS 4; pages are Home and Menu; content lives in `src/config/site.ts` and `src/config/menu.ts`.
- Current: menu browsing, opening hours, location and contact, testimonials.
- Planned (confirmed by the user): order ahead / pickup checkout, and an online shop for roasted beans / merch. Not yet built; payment and fulfilment details are undecided.
- Terminology: the shop is "Ember & Bean"; tagline "Small-batch coffee, slowly made."

## Brand Commitments

Name Ember & Bean and the tagline above. No other voice or identity constraints were stated.

## Evidence on Hand

All current content is placeholder / demo: business facts, menu, prices, hours, testimonials (Maya R., Daniel K., Priya S.) and stats (12 partner farms, 48h roast-to-cup, since 2019) are fictional. No real photography, logo or press exists in the repo; the current visuals use emoji. Future work may revise placeholder content freely but must not present invented claims as verified.

## Product Principles

- Answer the local's practical questions first: what's on the menu, what it costs, when it's open, where it is.
- Make the shop feel specific and rooted in its neighborhood, not like a generic café.
- Keep the path from interest to visit (and later, to ordering) short on a phone.
- Content is placeholder; structure the site so real details are easy to swap in.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No product-specific requirement stated; meet baseline WCAG AA for contrast, keyboard use and readable menu text.
